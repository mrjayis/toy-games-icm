Toy Game #0 · Method

How to Solve a Toy Game

You can't control the EV of your own decisions, only your opponent's. Prune the game tree of dominated options, find the decisions that are really mixed, then make your opponent's risk indifferent. One rule covers every frequency: point the red arrow at the opponent's risk, the yellow arrow at the pot before it, and the blue arrow at the pot after it.

Winning % = yellow / blue Losing % = red / blue Win : loss = yellow : red
Toy Game #1

Polarized vs Bluffcatcher

The bettor holds only the nuts or complete air. The caller holds medium-strength hands that beat bluffs but lose to value. Three formulas govern equilibrium: MDF, bluff ratio, and EV extraction.

MDF = 1/(1+s)
Bluff% = s/(1+2s)

Traps in Defender's Range

When the caller has hands that beat value bets, optimal sizing shrinks. The bettor's "nut advantage" determines the right bet size — bigger traps mean smaller bets.

Tournament Reality

ICM Breaks Nash

In cash games, Nash guarantees minimum EV protection. In tournaments, chip equity ≠ dollar equity. Pots are non-zero-sum: uninvolved players gain EV when you win chips.

Chip EV ≠ Dollar EV

Three Pillars of Toy Game Analysis

Pillar 1
Pot Odds

Minimum equity required to call. Sets the bluffcatcher's decision threshold. Formula: s/(1+2s)

Pillar 2
MDF

Minimum Defense Frequency — how often caller must defend to make bluffs unprofitable. Formula: 1/(1+s). It's set by the bettor's risk, not the caller's price.

Pillar 3
Expected Value

EV framework drives all equilibrium solutions. At equilibrium, both players are indifferent between their available actions.

1 · Where the Money Lives
Money at the table is in one of three places, and the pot is split between the two players' EVs

The bottom row is the pot. OOP EV + IP EV = pot (with no rake).

Risk means money leaving a stack and entering the pot. Once it's in the pot, it belongs to nobody until the hand is decided. All that's left to decide is how the pot gets split between the two EVs.

You can't control the EV of your own decisions. Whether your bluff makes money is up to your opponent: if they always call it's −EV, and if they always fold it's +EV. You can't choose how often they call. What you do control is the EV of their decisions, through how often you value bet and bluff.

So equilibrium is two players each making the other player indifferent. You point at your opponent's risk and choose your frequencies so that risk earns them exactly what their alternative does.

3 · Pruning the Game Tree — KK/33 vs 88
OOP holds KK or 33, IP holds 88, board makes KK > 88 > 33 · Start with every option (even open-folding), then remove dominated options until none are left
Step 0 / 5
Two kinds of domination

Strictly dominated: another option is always better.

Weakly dominated: another option is always at least as good, and sometimes better.

Repeat: every pruned branch can make an option that wasn't dominated before become dominated.

What's left to make indifferent

KK always jams: one option, no indifference needed.

88 facing a check: checks behind (betting is just as good, so this is a tie-break): no indifference needed.

33: jam or check-fold. Must be made indifferent by 88.

88 facing a jam: call or fold. Must be made indifferent by OOP.

The two equations

88 makes 33 indifferent: 33's jam risks J into a pot of P, so 88 calls P / (P + J) of the time.

OOP makes 88 indifferent: 88's call risks J into a pot of P + J, so OOP needs P + J value jams for every J bluff jams.

This only holds while 33 is actually mixing. If OOP has too few 33s to bluff at the right ratio, 33 always jams, 88 always folds, and jamming 33 is +EV, not $0.

The opponent's other option has to be folding or giving up. That's what makes "indifferent" mean "this risk earns $0".

Polarized outcomes

Value always wins when called, and bluffs never do. Once bluffs have equity or value can be outdrawn, the ratios shift.

The decision is really mixed

If one side can't reach the required ratio (too few bluffs, or too few calls), the other side stops being indifferent and plays a pure strategy.

Takeaway: Against a best-responding opponent (a nemesis), the bettor's EV is a tent peaking exactly at the GTO bluffing frequency — over-bluff and every extra bluff gets snapped off, under-bluff and your value bets stop getting paid. Tournament Theory

ICM & Nash Limitations

Why zero-sum Nash guarantees break down in tournament poker.

Zero-Sum vs Non-Zero-Sum

"These guarantees only apply to heads-up (HU) zero-sum games!"
— GTOWizard · The Limitations of Nash Equilibrium in ICM Spots
"In tournaments, the sum of payoffs between two players in a pot is no longer zero. Some of their $EV can get leaked to the rest of the field."
— GTOWizard · The Limitations of Nash Equilibrium in ICM Spots
GTO Guarantee ✓ Heads-Up Cash — Zero-Sum ✗ Tournament — Non-Zero-Sum
Opponent mistakes
Who benefits when your opponent plays badly?
 Their loss is your gain — all misdirected EV flows directly to you. Their loss leaks across the table. Other players gain EV without being in the hand.
Adding bet sizes
Does having more options always help you?
 More options can only help. A new bet size either gets used (improving EV) or gets ignored. A new bet size can open equilibria that are worse for you — the field captures the upside.
Playing GTO protects you
Does GTO guarantee a minimum outcome?
 Yes — GTO guarantees a minimum EV floor. Opponents can deviate, but they can only hurt themselves. No floor. Multiple Nash equilibria exist with different payoffs, and you can't force the best one.
Value of chips
Is each chip worth the same dollar amount?
 Yes — 1 chip equals the same dollar value at every stack size. No — doubling your stack less than doubles your equity. ICM bends the chip-to-dollar curve.
Sitting out a hand
Does your equity change while you're not in a pot?
 Never. Your stack stays the same; your equity stays the same. Always. Every elimination moves you up the payout ladder, changing your $EV without touching your chips.
"If the sum of payoffs (between the two active players) is not zero, things break!"
— GTOWizard · The Limitations of Nash Equilibrium in ICM Spots
"Nash Equilibrium still works and is well-defined in non-zero-sum games. Your opponents cannot gain EV against a GTO strategy. But the aforementioned guarantees no longer hold."
— GTOWizard · The Limitations of Nash Equilibrium in ICM Spots

Why MDF Shrinks: Under ICM, your chips at risk are worth more than the chips you'd win. Folding preserves equity relative to the field. This means opponents' bluffs become more profitable — they can bluff more, but you should still call less.
Bluff Frequency Adjustment
Leak-adjusted bluffing in ICM spots
Counterintuitive: Even though ICM callers call less, you shouldn't bluff proportionally more. When an opponent folds, they don't lose as much in ICM terms — their fold is less costly. Optimal bluff frequency may still decrease on the bubble.

Worked Example: Bubble with 25BB

Scenario

You hold 25BB on the money bubble. Opponent bets pot (1x) on the river. GTO says call with 50% of your range.

ICM Reality

ICM pressure ≈ 30–40%. Adjusted calling threshold: ~35%. Drop marginal bluffcatchers (weak pairs, medium draws).

Practical Rule

On bubble: tighten calling ranges by 15–20% relative to Nash. Keep value calls, fold marginal bluffcatchers.
